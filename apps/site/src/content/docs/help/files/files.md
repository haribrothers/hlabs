For everyone.

Choose **Files** in the Dock (or the tab bar on a phone) to see the files you keep on hlabs. Everyone with an hlabs account has a private **Home** folder that only they can open.

## Your places

The list on the left has every place you can open. On a phone, the same places are a row of buttons under the search field.

- **Recents:** the 50 files you changed most recently, newest first, with the folder each one is in.
- **Home:** your own folder. The first time you open Files, hlabs makes **Photos**, **Documents** and **Downloads** in it for you; folders you already have are left as they are.
- **Photos** and **Downloads:** those folders in your Home.
- **Shared with me:** the Shared folder everyone in the household can use. Admins always see it; family members see it if their admin turned on **See the Shared folder in Files** for them.

Admins also see:

- **This computer:** every person's Home folder (by their name) and Shared. A Home that was kept when someone was removed shows here with "deleted" and the date in its name.
- One row for each network drive you've connected. A drive that can't be reached has a grey dot; you can still choose it to try again.
- **App data:** the folder each installed app keeps its data in. You can look and download, but not change anything here, so running apps stay safe.
- How much of this computer's disk is used, at the bottom.

## Browse a folder

The open folder shows its folders first, then its files, each from A to Z. Under each folder is how many items it holds; under each file, its size. Photos and videos show a small picture of themselves; other files show an icon for their kind (a PDF, a spreadsheet, a zip file and so on).

- **Open a folder:** click it (tap it on a phone), or move to it with the arrow keys and press Return.
- **Go back up:** choose a folder in the path at the top, for example **Home** in **Home › Documents**. Deep folders fold the middle of the path into **…**; choose it to pick one of them. Your browser's Back and Forward buttons move between the folders you opened too.
- **Use the keyboard:** the arrow keys move between items; Return opens the one you're on.

At the bottom, hlabs says how many items the folder holds and how much space its files take, and who can see it: **Private to you** in your Home, or **Everyone who can see Shared can open this** in Shared.

If someone removes a folder while you have it open, Files says **This folder no longer exists**; choose **Go to Home** to carry on.

While a folder loads, Files shows grey placeholders where the items will be; the path at the top and the buttons still work. A network drive that is slow to answer adds **Still loading… this drive is slow to respond**. If the folder can't be loaded, choose **Try again**.

### On a phone

On a phone, Files has a large **Files** title with **+** and **Select** above it, then the search field, your places as a row of buttons, and the path to the open folder (for example **Home › Documents**). The folder is a list: each row shows the item's icon (or a small picture of a photo), its name, and how many items a folder holds or a file's size and when it changed. Tap a row to open it, and tap **•••** at the end of a row for what you can do with it. **+** offers **New folder** and **Upload**.

### List view and sorting

Choose the list button beside the search field to see the folder as a list with each item's **Name**, when it was **Modified** and its **Size** (for a folder, how many items it holds). Recent changes say **Yesterday**, **2 days ago** or **3 weeks ago**; older ones show the date.

Choose a column's name to sort by it, and choose it again to turn the order around; an arrow shows which column is sorted and which way. Folders always stay above files. Files remembers the view and the order in this browser, and the order applies to the grid too.

### Select several items

A click opens a file or folder. To work on several at once, choose **Select** beside the buttons at the top (or press and hold an item, or press **⌘A**, Ctrl+A on Windows and Linux). Every item then shows a tick box: click items to tick them, or hold **Shift** and click to tick everything between. **Select all** ticks the whole folder.

A bar says how many are ticked, with what you can do with them: **Download** saves them (several come as one zip file), and **Share** sends files with your device's share sheet where it can. At the bottom, Files adds up how much the ticked files take. Choose **✕** or press **Esc** to stop selecting; opening another folder stops it too. On a phone, tap **Select**: tap rows to tick them, **Select all** ticks the whole folder, the buttons along the bottom (where the tab bar was) act on the ticked items, and **Done** stops selecting.

### The right-click menu

Right-click a file or folder (or select it and press **Shift+F10** or the Menu key) for everything you can do with it: **Open**, **Open with** an app that uses its folder, **Share…** (files, with your device's share sheet), **Copy link**, **Download**, **Rename…**, **Move to…**, **Duplicate** and **Move to Trash**. **Copy link** copies an address to the item; only people who can open its folder can use it. **Duplicate** makes a copy beside it, such as **Lease agreement copy.pdf**. While you're selecting, right-clicking a ticked item makes the menu act on all ticked items. In a folder you can only read, the options that change things are greyed out.

On a phone, tap **•••** on any file or folder for the same options: **Open**, **Share** and **Download** across the top, then the rest, with **Move to Trash** on its own at the bottom.

### Move items to another folder

Right-click an item and choose **Move to…**, or tick several and choose **Move to…** (on a phone, **Move** along the bottom), or open a file and choose **•••** › **Move to…**. Pick a folder in the list (it opens at the folder you're in, marked **current**) and choose **Move to** *folder*. On a phone this fills the screen, with **Cancel** at the top and **New folder** beside **Move to** *folder* at the bottom. **+ New folder** makes a folder inside the one you picked. You can also drag items onto a folder, or onto a place on the left.

Moving within the same disk is instant, and the message at the bottom has **Undo** to put them back. Moving to another disk, such as a network drive, or more than 1 GB, carries on in the background with a bar showing how far it got; the items leave where they were only once their copy is checked. If something with the same name is already there, Files asks whether to skip it, keep both (the moved one gets a number) or replace the one that's there (it goes to the Trash).

### Delete files and folders

Right-click an item and choose **Move to Trash**, or tick several and choose **Move to Trash** (on a phone, **Trash** along the bottom), press **Delete** or **Backspace**, or open a file and choose **•••** › **Move to Trash**. They go to your Trash, and the message at the bottom has **Undo** for a few seconds. Something deleted from Shared goes to the Trash of whoever deleted it.

Trash only covers this computer. On a network drive, Files asks **Delete … for good?** first, because those items can't be brought back.

### Get something back from the Trash

Choose **Trash** on the left (on a phone, the **Trash** button in the row of places). It lists what you deleted, newest first, with the folder it came from, when you deleted it and how many days are left before it's deleted for good (30 in all). Choose **Restore** to put an item back where it was (on a phone, the round arrow at the end of its row), or **Restore all**. If its folder is gone, Files makes it again; if something with the same name is there now, the restored one gets a number, such as **Lease (2).pdf**. Everyone has their own Trash: nobody else sees what you deleted.

Items are deleted for good 30 days after you deleted them. To free the space sooner, choose **Empty trash**: Files says how many items and how much space, and asks first, because they can't be brought back. Only your own Trash is emptied.

### Rename a file or folder

Move to an item with the arrow keys and press **F2**, right-click it and choose **Rename…**, or open a file and choose **•••** › **Rename…**. Type the new name and press Return. For a file, only the part before its ending (such as **.pdf**) changes, so apps still know how to open it. Names can't be empty, contain **/** or match something already in the folder.

If an app keeps its data in a folder (or a folder inside it), Files says so before renaming it, because the app would stop finding it there; choose **Rename anyway** only if you'll point the app at the new name.

## Make a folder

Choose **New folder** at the top (on a phone, **+** › **New folder**), type a name (or keep **Untitled folder**) and choose **Create**, or press Return. The new folder is selected so you can open it straight away.

A name can't be empty, contain "/" or be very long, and there can't already be a file or folder with that name in the same place; Files tells you which one it is so you can choose another name. In places you can't change, such as App data, **New folder** and **Upload** are greyed out and say **This folder is read only**.

## Find a file

Type at least two letters of its name in **Search** at the top. Files looks in the folder you have open and every folder inside it, and shows what it finds with the folder each one is in, for example **Home › Documents › Bills**. Upper and lower case don't matter.

Files shows up to 200 results; if there are more, it says **Showing the first 200 results**, so type a little more of the name. A very large folder may say **Showing the first results** when it couldn't look everywhere in time. Press Esc, or clear the search, to go back to the folder.

## Upload files

Open the folder you want them in, choose **Upload** (on a phone, **+** › **Upload**) and pick one or more files, or drag files from your computer onto the folder (Files says **Drop to upload to** and the folder's name). Folders can't be uploaded yet: pick the files inside instead.

The **Uploads** panel shows each file: **Waiting**, how far along it is (for example **62% · 1.1 of 1.8 GB**) or **Done**, and about how long is left. Files go one at a time, in the order you chose them. When they're all done, the panel closes after a few seconds and says how many were uploaded. If there isn't enough space on the disk for a file (hlabs keeps 1 GB free), it says **Not enough space** and goes on to the next.

- **Pause** stops after the piece being sent; **Resume** carries on from where it stopped.
- If the connection drops, hlabs keeps trying for about half a minute. If it still can't, the upload pauses with **Paused · connection lost**; choose **Resume** when you're back online.
- **Cancel all** stops every upload that hasn't finished and removes what was sent of them. Files already uploaded stay.
- If you close the page or it reloads during an upload, Files says the uploads were interrupted. Choose **Choose files again** and pick the same files: they carry on where they stopped. Unfinished uploads are removed after a day.

### When a file with the same name is already there

Files asks what to do: **Skip** leaves the file that's there and doesn't upload yours; **Keep both** saves yours with a number, for example **Report (2).pdf**; **Replace** puts yours in its place and moves the old one to the Trash. Tick **Do this for every conflict** to use the same answer for the rest of the files you're uploading.

## Look at a file

Open a file to see it without downloading it. Photos show at the size of the window, videos play with the usual controls, PDFs open in your browser's own viewer (on a phone, use **Download** instead), and text files such as notes, lists and code show their first 1 MB. Other kinds of file, such as spreadsheets, zip files and iPhone HEIC photos, show their icon with **Download**.

If an app you can use keeps its files in that folder (for example Immich for your Photos), **Open with** lists it under Details; choose it to open the app in a new tab.

Use the arrows at the sides, or the Left and Right arrow keys, to see the previous or next file in the folder. **Details** shows the file's size, its width and height for photos and videos, when it last changed and where it is; choose the location to open that folder. Press Esc or choose **×** to close.

## Download a file

Open the file and choose **Download** at the top. The file keeps its name. Big downloads, such as videos, can pick up where they stopped if the connection drops.

## Connect a network drive

For admins. A NAS or another computer that shares folders on your network (SMB or NFS) can show up in Files, and apps can keep their files on it.

1. In Files, choose **Connect a drive** under Locations.
2. Under **Found on your network**, choose your NAS and then the share you want, for example **Media**. Many NAS boxes don't list their shares to visitors: then type your username and password and choose **Show shares** to see the ones your account can open, or type the share's name under **Share name**. If the NAS itself isn't listed, type its address under **Or enter an address**: `smb://nas.local/Media`, `\\nas.local\Media` or `nfs://nas.local/volume1/media`.
3. For SMB, type the username and password you use for the NAS. For a share anyone on your network can open, leave them empty. NFS doesn't need them.
4. Leave **Let apps use this drive** on if apps should be able to keep folders on it (you choose which ones), and **Reconnect automatically after restart** on so hlabs connects it again by itself.
5. Choose **Connect**. hlabs tries the drive first and says what went wrong if it can't: **Wrong username or password**, **Can't reach** the NAS (check it's on and on the same network), or that there's no share with that name.

The drive then shows under Locations, for example **NAS · Media**. The password is kept in your computer's keychain, never in hlabs's database.

### When a drive can't be reached

If the NAS is off or the network drops, the drive's row gets a grey dot and opening it says **Can't reach** and its name. Choose **Try again** once it's back. With **Reconnect automatically** on, hlabs also tries every minute by itself.

### Disconnect a drive

Right-click the drive in the list (or press and hold on a touch screen) and choose **Disconnect**. The files on the drive aren't deleted; hlabs just stops using it and forgets its password. If an app keeps folders on the drive, Files says which one: choose another folder for that app first.
