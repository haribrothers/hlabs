## New

- Back up your apps' data and everyone's Home folders every night, encrypted, to a NAS, a drive or cloud storage, and restore an app or a folder from any restore point.
- Files: browse, upload, preview, rename, move and download your files, with a Trash you can undo from.
- The App Store has 100 apps, with categories, search filters, an updates list with **Update all**, and automatic updates for the apps you allow.
- Add other app libraries, or start your own with the `hlabs library` command, and copy a built-in app into it to change it.
- Notifications: a bell on Home with what needs you, such as updates, failed backups and apps that stopped.
- Make Home yours: rearrange apps, add widgets with live data from your apps, and pin favourites to the Dock.
- App settings: change an app's settings and secrets, its web address and port, which folders and networks it can use, and whether it needs your hlabs login. See how much processor, memory, disk and network it uses, with the last 24 hours on a chart.
- Appearance: a wallpaper, an accent colour, Home options, and less transparency or motion.
- Storage: see what uses your disk, free space from app images nothing uses, and empty everyone's Trash.
- Advanced: hlabs's logs, a diagnostics file to share when you ask for help, the list of everything hlabs connects to, beta updates, restart hlabs, and reset it to how it came.
- Share an app on the internet at a name of its own through Cloudflare Tunnel, with your hlabs login and two-factor in front, and see who visits. You can also reach the dashboard itself that way; changes that could lock you out only work at home.
- On a phone, hlabs has its own layout with a tab bar, and you can add it to your home screen. On a tablet, touch works everywhere.

## Fixed

- Backups, Files and uninstalling hlabs were listed in 0.6.0-beta.1 but hidden in the app. They're here now.

## Before you update

This is a beta: it may have bugs, so keep a copy of anything you can't lose. Sharing apps on the internet is new; turn it off from the menu bar or **Settings › Network** if anything looks wrong. The Mac version isn't signed by Apple yet, so the first time you open it macOS asks first: choose **Open Anyway** in System Settings › Privacy & Security.
